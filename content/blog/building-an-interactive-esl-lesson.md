An interactive lesson should not become a collection of buttons. It should still feel like a teacher made deliberate choices about explanation, sequence, challenge, and encouragement.

## Begin with an outcome

Write what learners should be able to do at the end. Keep it observable: explain a preference, use a tense in context, notice a listening detail, or respond politely in a conversation.

## Give each slide one job

Use a clear slide title and let the content block do one meaningful thing. A short explanation can lead to a matching activity. A dialogue can lead to a speaking response. A reading passage can lead to inference or vocabulary work.

## Vary practice with purpose

The best interaction depends on the skill. Matching can help connect vocabulary and meaning. Reordering can make sentence structure visible. Oral response makes room for a learner's own voice. Use an activity because it supports the outcome, not because it looks busy.
